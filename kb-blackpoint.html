IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Blackpoint’s CompassOne platform gives a customer team visibility into protected assets, security findings, and the work performed by Blackpoint’s response service.

Use this as a guide to reviewing the service and its evidence. General MDR concepts are in EDR, MDR, and XDR. CompassOne capabilities and labels depend on the customer’s enabled services and permissions; use the current partner documentation for exact click paths.

What to look for in the platform

Area of workWhat you need to establish
Customer / tenant contextThe organization whose data and actions you are viewing. MSPs support multiple customers.
Asset inventoryWhich endpoints are present and covered, and whether their information is current.
Detection and response findingsThe affected entities, evidence, urgency, response already performed, and outstanding recommendations.
IntegrationsWhich services are connected and healthy, including SentinelOne when configured.
Cloud posture and identity findingsThe relevant Microsoft 365 or identity coverage when those services are enabled.
ReportingThe record of findings and actions that supports customer follow-up.

Review a SentinelOne-related escalation

Blackpoint documents an integration that monitors SentinelOne alerts, investigates them, performs supported response, and adds resolution notes back in SentinelOne. Start by reading those notes. If the provider already terminated a process or isolated an endpoint, confirm the outcome and coordinate remaining work instead of repeating the action blindly.

  1. Match the Blackpoint finding to the customer, device, and SentinelOne detection ID.
  2. Read the timeline: what was observed, what was judged malicious, and what the SOC already did.
  3. Identify remaining tasks such as checking an account, patching software, restoring a device, or communicating with the customer.
  4. Confirm who owns each task and when restoration is allowed.
  5. Record your validation in the customer’s incident or service ticket.

Separate service status from recovery

In a fictional case, Blackpoint reports that a malicious process was stopped on FIN-LT-07. Your remaining work may include confirming the affected employee can use a replacement device, checking related account activity, and arranging remediation with the incident owner. The provider’s completed containment does not automatically mean the user’s business service is restored.

Before closing the customer ticket, verify the required remediation and monitoring are complete. Before releasing isolation, check the recovery criteria agreed by the incident owner. Preserve the provider’s findings and your own validation so the next analyst can see why the decision was made.

Check coverage before assuming visibility

A platform can only analyze data its sensors and integrations supply. Confirm that a device is onboarded, an integration is healthy, and the relevant service is included. Do not assume that “Blackpoint” means every firewall, mailbox, cloud application, and endpoint is automatically visible.

Questions worth asking the service owner

Which customers and devices are covered? Which integrations are enabled? Which actions can the provider take immediately? Who handles the customer’s recovery and communication? These answers make an escalation actionable.

PUT IT INTO WORDS

Check your understanding

Blackpoint reports successful containment. Why might the customer ticket still need work?

Show an example answer

Containment stops or limits the threat. The customer may still need remediation, account investigation, service recovery, and communication. Read the provider’s evidence, confirm the action outcome, and assign the remaining work before closing the ticket.
