NOTE 04 / Networking

VLANs & enforcement boundaries_

Study basis: Concept study

THE PRINCIPLE

A VLAN separates Layer 2 broadcast domains. Security policy must control the routed paths between them.

THE CONNECTION

A logical grouping of ports describes the configuration. A separate broadcast domain explains the result and the opportunity to enforce inter-segment policy.

Logical separation on shared hardware

A VLAN groups endpoints into a Layer 2 broadcast domain on shared switching infrastructure. An ordinary access port places an endpoint's traffic into one VLAN. A trunk carries multiple VLANs between network devices, commonly using 802.1Q tags. Native-VLAN and tagging behavior depend on configuration.

VLANs can span switches. Two devices may use the same switch chassis while remaining logically separated; conversely, one VLAN may extend across several switches.

VLAN versus subnet

A VLAN is a Layer 2 concept. An IP subnet is a Layer 3 addressing concept. A conventional routed design assigns a distinct subnet to each VLAN. Creating a VLAN does not calculate a prefix, assign endpoint addresses, or create a gateway. Choosing a different IP range alone does not create a VLAN.

Workstations might use VLAN 10 and 192.168.10.0/24; guests might use VLAN 20 and 192.168.20.0/24. Both can share an internet egress address through NAT. A VLAN does not require private addressing, and its identifier says nothing about public-address allocation.

Where enforcement happens

Traffic switched directly within a VLAN does not ordinarily traverse the internet-edge firewall. That firewall cannot apply policy to packets it never receives. In a routed inter-VLAN design, traffic crosses a Layer 3 interface, where an ACL or an in-path firewall can permit required services and deny unnecessary access.

Guest VLAN→Routing + access policy→Server VLAN

Routing on a Layer 3 switch does not automatically place a separate firewall in the path. Verify the actual route and applied controls. VLANs with unrestricted inter-VLAN routing separate broadcasts without delivering the intended application-access restrictions.

How a DMZ fits

A DMZ is a design for placing public-facing services in a controlled segment. It may use a VLAN and firewall rules. Internet users can reach an exposed web service without receiving general access to internal workstations. If the web service requires an internal database, permit only its required source, destination, and service. The VLAN creates the segment; policy governs its paths; “DMZ” describes its purpose.

Recall check

Does creating a guest VLAN guarantee that guests cannot reach a server VLAN?

Reveal the reasoning

No. The routed path and its ACL or firewall rules determine allowed access. Verify the enforcement point and test both permitted and denied flows.
