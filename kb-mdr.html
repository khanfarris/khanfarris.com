NOTE 09 / Security operations

EDR, MDR, and XDR_

Concept Concept study

IN PLAIN ENGLISH

EDR watches devices. XDR brings signals from several security areas together. MDR provides people and processes to investigate and respond using those tools.

Start with the problem

A user opens an attachment and something suspicious runs on their laptop. You need to know what ran, whether it caused harm, whether other accounts or devices are involved, and who will respond. EDR, XDR, and MDR address different parts of this work. They are not three mutually exclusive products or a required sequence of upgrades.

Understand each role

TermWhat it contributesExample
Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR)Software records device activity such as processes, files, and network connections. It can detect threats and perform supported response actions.SentinelOne or Microsoft Defender for Endpoint.
Extended Detection and Response (XDR)A platform links related signals across areas such as endpoints, email, identities, and cloud applications.Connecting a phishing email, a suspicious process, and a risky account sign-in into an investigation.
Managed Detection and Response (MDR)A service supplies analysts and an agreed process for monitoring, investigation, and response. It uses its own and/or integrated tools.A Blackpoint SOC analyst investigates an integrated SentinelOne alert and takes action within the contracted scope.

An endpoint is a device such as a laptop or server. Telemetry means the recorded activity it sends. A SOC, or security operations center, is the team and function that monitors and responds to threats. MDR adds that operational service; XDR describes a technology approach, not automatically a staffed team.

How an EDR can feed an MDR service

An endpoint agent sends activity and alerts to its product’s platform. A supported integration can share those alerts and relevant context with an MDR provider, often through an API: a structured connection between products. The provider’s analysts investigate with the available evidence and their own sensors or connected services. Depending on permissions, they may use that integration to stop a process or isolate a device.

For a concrete example, Blackpoint documents a SentinelOne integration that monitors alerts, supports response, and writes resolution information back into SentinelOne. This requires a configured integration and service coverage. It is not a guarantee that every raw endpoint event or firewall log is included in every MDR plan.

Where XDR and a SIEM fit

An XDR platform can connect the endpoint alert to an email delivered to the same person and a suspicious sign-in. A SIEM provides broader log collection, search, and detection across configured sources. Their capabilities can overlap and integrate. Microsoft Sentinel is a separate product from SentinelOne; Sentinel and Defender XDR can share an investigation experience.

What an analyst still needs to do

  • Confirm the customer, affected device, user, timestamp, and evidence behind the alert.
  • Read what automatic response or the MDR provider already completed. “Detected” is different from “successfully contained.”
  • Determine scope: other devices, mailbox activity, account use, and business impact.
  • Follow the response plan. Urgent containment can happen while investigation continues when the evidence and authority justify it.
  • Document actions, owners, outstanding tasks, and the evidence needed before restoring access.
Do not equate the labels

MDR does not simply mean “a place to view firewall logs.” The defining feature is the managed investigation and response service. Verify the actual data sources and responsibilities in the deployed service.

Why would an organization use SentinelOne and Blackpoint together instead of choosing only one?

SentinelOne supplies endpoint visibility and response controls.
