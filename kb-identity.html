NOTE 13 / Identity & access

Risky sign-ins_

Concept Scenario study

IN PLAIN ENGLISH

A risky sign-in is an authentication event with signals that deserve investigation. The signal helps prioritize work; the surrounding evidence determines whether an account is compromised.

Understand the signal

An account signs in from Colorado and soon afterward appears to sign in from another continent. That travel seems implausible, so an identity system may flag it. An impossible-travel style detection compares time and apparent locations; other signals include unfamiliar properties or known malicious infrastructure. Names and logic vary by provider.

A network location is normally inferred from the IP address. A VPN, mobile provider, cloud proxy, or inaccurate location database can change that appearance without the user physically moving. Conversely, a familiar location is not proof of safety. Attackers may use nearby infrastructure or an already-established session.

Read the sign-in as a record

FieldWhy it matters
User and tenantIdentify the actual account and organization. Similar display names can mislead.
Time and resultEstablish whether authentication succeeded and keep the time zone consistent.
Application / resourceIdentify what the account was trying to access.
IP, device, client, and locationCompare with the account’s usual pattern and known company infrastructure.
Authentication detailsSee how MFA or another requirement was satisfied; a success can rely on an earlier claim or session.
Conditional Access resultIdentify which policies applied, which requirement failed, or why access was allowed.

Work through the investigation

  1. Confirm the alert’s evidence and whether suspicious access succeeded. Compare nearby sign-ins, devices, and expected VPN use.
  2. Contact the user through a trusted channel to verify relevant activity, without relying only on their recollection.
  3. Look for subsequent resource activity: mailbox rules, sent messages, downloaded files, changed authentication methods, or new permissions where logs are available.
  4. Assess impact and urgency. Use the incident plan to contain credible active compromise while continuing investigation.
  5. Document the evidence, alternative explanations, actions, and the remaining uncertainty.

Containment is more than a password reset

Authorized response can include blocking sign-in, resetting credentials, revoking sessions, and reviewing registered authentication methods or suspicious application access. These actions have different effects. Revocation is not a promise that every existing token or application session immediately becomes unusable; token lifetime, application behavior, and supported continuous evaluation matter. Verify the observed outcome.

Do not delete every authentication method automatically because a location looked unusual. Determine what was changed or abused and use the approved recovery procedure. Similarly, changing a password does not remove a malicious forwarding rule or identify which data was accessed.

Use the right source of evidence

Entra sign-in logs explain authentication. Email and cloud application audit records show relevant activity within those services. Endpoint tools show device activity. Intune provides device-management and compliance information; it is not a universal record of everything an attacker did.

PUT IT INTO WORDS

Check your understanding

Two successful sign-ins appear far apart within minutes. What would make you more or less concerned?

Show an example answer

Compare known VPN or proxy use, device and client details, authentication and policy results, and subsequent activity. A user-confirmed VPN with expected behavior may explain the location shift. Unexpected MFA changes, mailbox forwarding, or unfamiliar resource access increase concern and may justify prompt containment under the response plan.
